Time of useful consciousness by altitude

The line shows the middle of the published range. It starts at 20,000 ft, and the scale is logarithmic so that seconds and minutes fit on one chart.

Key takeaway

Oxygen availability falls steadily as you climb. Above 20,000 ft the time you can act sensibly shrinks from minutes to seconds, so cabin pressurization and oxygen equipment are safety-critical, not optional extras.

Think about it

A cabin loses pressure at 35,000 ft. Use the tool to find how long people have to act. What does that mean for emergency procedures and for how crews are trained?

Show a discussion point

At 35,000 ft the tool shows about 30 to 60 seconds, which is very little time. That is why crews put on their own oxygen first and start a descent promptly, and why the drill is practised regularly.

For learning only. Values are typical figures for healthy adults and vary from person to person, so they must not be used for real flight-safety decisions. Airliner cabins are pressurized to roughly 6,000 to 8,000 ft, so passengers normally stay in the first stage. Time of useful consciousness between published altitudes is estimated.
